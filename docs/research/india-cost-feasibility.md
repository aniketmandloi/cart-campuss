# What India purchase-cost and affordability estimates can reliably calculate

Research date: 2026-10-22. This note answers the research ticket [What can India purchase-cost and affordability estimates reliably calculate?](https://github.com/aniketmandloi/cart-campuss/issues/3). It records texts that were read, and it separates those texts from rates whose currency through 22 October 2026 was not established. It does not close the ticket.

## Recommendation

The first release calculates two kinds of total, and no tax of its own.

1. **Domestic, new or used, any Indian state.** Start from the seller's quoted rupee price. Keep the quote's own label (ex-showroom, on-road, or unspecified). Add only insurance, registration, and seller charges that the user types in. Show a total only as the sum of the quote and those entered lines.
2. **Foreign listing.** Show the listing in its source currency with import status **unknown / requires review**. Offer a separate hypothetical sheet: the user types each auction, freight, duty, broker, and inland line, and may type an exchange rate and the date of that rate. The sheet sums those entries and labels the sum a hypothetical itemized scenario.

Automatic GST, compensation cess, state motor-vehicle tax, registration fees, customs duty, insurance premiums, and import-eligibility decisions stay out of both totals. A later release may calculate one state's tax only after a single rule pack names the instrument, its effective date, the tax base in that instrument's words, the full rate table, and a `verified_on` date on which that text was confirmed still to be the operative text. Delhi, Maharashtra, and Karnataka do not have such a pack. Partial or older schedules are recorded below so they are not mistaken for one.

Affordability is a budget scenario: a user-supplied nominal interest rate, tenure, down payment, and optional monthly budget. It is not a lender approval, not a salary multiple, and not a live bank rate.

## Price basis

"Ex-showroom" is not a term in the GST rate notification read for this note. In a dealer quote it is the price of the vehicle before state registration and insurance. That dealer price is the amount the buyer pays the supplier. Central GST is imposed on the taxable value and is indicated on the tax invoice; the buyer's price already contains it. Adding 18% or 40% to an ex-showroom quote counts GST twice.

An on-road quote is a seller's bundled figure. The estimate shows that figure. Registration and insurance are added again only when the user enters them and the basis says they sit outside the quote.

When the listing does not say whether the figure is ex-showroom, on-road, or something else, the estimate shows the quoted amount and leaves registration, insurance, and seller charges as open lines. It does not invent a bundle.

The same rule covers a used domestic price. Notification No. 12/2025-Central Tax (Rate) shows that old and used vehicles remain under a separate 2018 margin notification, retargeted at the new schedules. The margin percentages in that 2018 notification were not read. A used asking price is therefore taken as the seller's price, not rebuilt from a margin formula.

## GST and compensation cess

These are classification facts about the 17 September 2025 rate notification. They are not multipliers for a first-release quote.

Gazette text read: Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate), G.S.R. 641(E), 17 September 2025, in the Gazette of India, [CG-DL-E-17092025-266209](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/266209.pdf). It supersedes Notification No. 1/2017-Central Tax (Rate) and comes into force on 22 September 2025. It sets central tax at:

- 2.5% for Schedule I
- 9% for Schedule II
- 20% for Schedule III

In the English text of that gazette, the heading 87 entry for electrically operated vehicles, and the fuel-cell motor-vehicle entry, fall after the Schedule I heading and before the Schedule II heading. Petrol, LPG, or CNG vehicles of engine capacity not exceeding 1,200 cc and length not exceeding 4,000 mm, diesel vehicles not exceeding 1,500 cc and 4,000 mm, ambulances, three-wheelers, a certified orthopaedic-disability class, and the smaller hybrid classes are Schedule II entries. Schedule III then taxes heading 8703 motor cars and other passenger vehicles except the goods named in Schedule II serials 533 to 539, and it taxes the larger hybrid classes (spark-ignition hybrids above 1,200 cc or 4,000 mm; compression-ignition hybrids above 1,500 cc or 4,000 mm).

The GST Council's published FAQ, posted by PIB on 3 September 2025 and hosted at [gstcouncil.gov.in](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/faq_0.pdf), describes the combined GST rate as 18% for small cars and 40% with no compensation cess for mid-size and large cars. Small cars there mean petrol, LPG, or CNG up to 1,200 cc and 4,000 mm, and diesel up to 1,500 cc and 4,000 mm. The same FAQ says three-wheelers under 8703, ambulances, and 8702 vehicles for ten or more persons are 18%, reduced from 28%. The Council's car answers treat the GST rate as double the central rate in this notification (9% central described as 18% GST; 20% central described as 40% GST). On that convention, a Schedule I vehicle entry is a 5% GST classification. The FAQ extract that was read does not itself contain a sentence saying electric cars are 5%.

One FAQ sentence is wider than the schedule and leaves a gap. It says vehicles exceeding 1,500 cc or 4,000 mm are 40%, and it also gives a utility-vehicle test of engine above 1,500 cc, length above 4,000 mm, and ground clearance of 170 mm or more. A petrol vehicle of 1,400 cc and length under 4,000 mm is outside the Schedule II small-car entry and outside that FAQ sentence. The schedule's residual 8703 entry is the operative classification. Ground clearance was not part of the schedule rows recovered from the gazette text. Missing engine capacity or length means the GST class is unknown. The first release does not need the class, because it does not recompute GST.

Compensation cess on the car serials of the 2017 cess notification is nil from 22 September 2025. Gazette text read in [CG-DL-E-17092025-266219](https://egazette.gov.in/WriteReadData/2025/266219.pdf): Notification No. 02/2025-Compensation Cess (Rate), G.S.R. 659(E), 17 September 2025, substitutes "Nil" for the rate column of serials 2, 3, 4, 4A, 4B, 39, 40, 41, 42, 47, 48, 50, 51, 52, 52A, 52B, 53, 54, and 55, with effect from 22 September 2025. The same gazette's note says the principal notification was last amended by Notification No. 3/2023-Compensation Cess (Rate), dated 26 July 2023. This research did not re-read the 2017 descriptions of those serials. The Council FAQ is the source for the statement that the 40% car rate applies without cess. The pre-22 September 2025 stack of 28% GST plus compensation cess is the wrong levy for a supply on or after that date. It is also the wrong thing to add to a quote that already includes whatever tax the seller charged.

The same gazette contains Notification No. 12/2025-Central Tax (Rate), G.S.R. 650(E). From 22 September 2025 it amends Notification No. 8/2018-Central Tax (Rate) so that the old reference to Schedule IV of Notification No. 1/2017-Central Tax (Rate) points instead to Schedule II or Schedule III of Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate). Its note says Notification No. 8/2018 was last amended by Notification No. 04/2025-Central Tax (Rate), dated 16 January 2025. Neither 8/2018 nor 04/2025 was read. Used-vehicle margin rates are unknown.

Two later amendments of Notification No. 9/2025 were read from republications, not from the gazette. [Notification No. 19/2025-Central Tax (Rate), 31 December 2025](https://www.gstindiaonline.com/pages/gstlegislation/GST-Nfy/Central%20Tax%20Rate/2025/19-2025-CTR.pdf), effective 1 February 2026, inserts tobacco and pan-masala entries and omits Schedule VII. The [GST Council's December 2025 newsletter](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-01/monthly_newsletter_december_2025_1_0.pdf) describes that amendment as the tobacco and pan-masala change. [Notification No. 01/2026-Central Tax (Rate), 30 April 2026](https://www.gstindiaonline.com/pages/gstlegislation/GST-Nfy/Central%20Tax%20Rate/2026/01-2026-CTR-Eng.pdf), effective 1 May 2026, changes beverage tariff codes in Schedules I and III. Its note says Notification No. 9/2025 was last amended by Notification No. 19/2025. Neither amendment text changes the heading 8703 rows. That is not a certificate that no other rate notification exists between 1 May 2026 and 22 October 2026. The CBIC PDF links given on the [PIB notification-index page](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/archiveContent.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=276214) returned an HTML shell on 22 October 2026, so the gazette above is the text actually read for the 17 September 2025 rates.

Council FAQ question 7 says the IGST on imported goods follows the notified GST rates except where IGST is separately exempted. That sentence does not produce a landed-cost total. Basic customs duty, cesses, and the customs valuation base were not assembled.

## State tax and registration fees

No state rate is reliable enough to calculate. Each source below was read far enough to see the gap, and then left unused.

**Delhi.** The Transport Department's [tax-rate page](https://transport.delhi.gov.in/transport/tax-rate-0), read on 22 October 2026, says private and non-commercial rates are "as per Copy of Notification Attached." That attachment was not in the page HTML. The page body publishes annual rupee rates for commercial passenger and goods vehicles, and a sentence that diesel-propelled vehicles are charged an extra 25% of the applicable tax. The sentence sits with the commercial tables. It was not read as a private-car rule. The [registration page](https://transport.delhi.gov.in/transport/vehicle-registration) says a vehicle may be driven in a public place only after registration under section 39 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, and it lists one-time road tax "as applicable," a prescribed registration fee, and an insurance policy or cover note among the documents for a new private vehicle. It does not state the private-car tax percentage or the fee amount.

**Maharashtra.** The India Code PDF of the Maharashtra Motor Vehicles Tax Act, 1958, retrieved 22 October 2026 from [indiacode.nic.in](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/20155/1/The_maharashtra_motor_vehicles_tax_act.pdf), is a consolidation, not a currency certificate. In that text, "cost of vehicle" for a vehicle manufactured in India is the purchase-invoice cost and includes basic manufacturing cost, CGST, IGST, and compensation cess. For a vehicle imported into India it is the landed value: Customs Act assessable value, customs duty, additional duty if any, IGST, and compensation cess. Discount is added back into that cost. One-time tax on motor cars is by reference to the Third Schedule. The schedule text says its petrol, diesel, and factory-fitted CNG/LPG slabs were substituted by Mah. 50 of 2017. Those slabs are 11/12/13% (petrol), 13/14/15% (diesel), and 7/8/9% (factory CNG/LPG) across cost bands of Rs 10 lakh and Rs 20 lakh. The Act text also contains a twice-rate rule for specified non-individual and imported cars, a proviso that the twice-rate not exceed 20% of cost, and a proviso capping tax at Rs 20 lakh. The PDF's amendment list includes acts through 2018, and the car-slab footnote stops at Mah. 50 of 2017.

That 2017 table is not the operative text for 22 October 2026 on the evidence in hand. A bill hosted by PRS, with a statement of objects dated 18 March 2025, proposes raising the one-time-tax ceiling to Rs 30 lakh, increasing the CNG/LPG rate by one percentage point, and taxing electric vehicles above Rs 30 lakh at 6%, among other changes. The statement says the bill seeks those changes. Enactment, commencement, and any later deletion of the electric-vehicle limb were not read from a Maharashtra Act or gazette. The India Code percentages, the Rs 20 lakh cap, and the bill's proposed Rs 30 lakh cap are all unused.

The invoice definition still matters for any future pack: Maharashtra's statutory cost, in the consolidation that was read, already includes GST and cess. A pack that applies a percentage to a pre-GST ex-factory price, or that adds GST on top of the invoice, uses the wrong base. Import status also changes the base in that text, from invoice cost to landed value. A foreign hammer price is not that base.

**Karnataka.** No official current lifetime-tax table was read. A Karnataka Legislative Council bill, [LA Bill No. 50 of 2024](https://kla.kar.nic.in/council/house/bills/154/50.pdf), inserts an additional cess of Rs 500 on a two-wheeler and Rs 1,000 on a motor car for non-transport vehicles, and it comes into force on a date the State Government notifies. The bill's annexed extract of section 3A also recites an 11% cess on the tax levied under section 3. The extract is the bill's annexure, and the additional flat cess has a commencement notification this research did not find. Percentage tables circulating as a 2026 gazette were seen only on secondary hosts and were not used.

**Central registration fees.** G.S.R. 492(E), 15 July 2021, inserts vintage-registration rows in CMVR rule 81: Rs 20,000 to register a vintage motor vehicle and Rs 5,000 to re-register or renew. The English text was read from a Mizoram Transport Department copy of gazette CG-DL-E-16072021-228336. The direct eGazette URL for that number returned HTTP 500 on 22 October 2026. Ordinary (non-vintage) rule 81 fee rows were not read from a current consolidation. The India Code CMVR PDF retrieved the same day has no rule 81A. The 2021 rupee amounts are what that amendment inserted. They were not confirmed unchanged through 22 October 2026, so they are not a default fee.

## Insurance and seller charges

Insurance is a registration document on the Delhi page above, and a vintage application must be accompanied by a policy under the 2021 rules below. The premium is the insurer's price. The [GST Council / PIB press release](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/press_release_press_information_bureau.pdf) read for this note exempts individual life and health insurance. It does not exempt motor insurance, and it does not state a motor-insurance GST rate. The estimate therefore keeps motor insurance as an entered quote. It does not mark that quote exempt, and it does not gross it up by an assumed rate.

Handling, accessories, extended warranty, a high-security plate, and FASTag have no national price in the texts read. Each is an entered line. Whether a charge is already inside the quoted price depends on the price basis above.

Tax collected at source on a motor-vehicle sale was not re-read from the Income-tax Act or a Finance Act as of 22 October 2026. The estimate does not insert a TCS percentage or a threshold. A user may add a cash-timing line. The line is labeled as money that may be credited against the buyer's income tax when the law requires the seller to collect it, and a blank line is not treated as tax collected.

## Import eligibility before any rupee total

Paying an auction, a broker, or customs duty does not establish that the vehicle can be registered or driven in India. The first release keeps foreign listings visible and marks import status unknown.

**Vintage route, as amended on 7 February 2025.** The official DGFT file is a one-page scan, [Notification No. 58/2024-25](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/646b345f-2157-4549-9ae9-94ee7f8c3a71/Notification%20no.%2058%20dated%2007.02.2025.pdf), dated 7 February 2025. OCR of that scan, not a born-digital gazette, is the text relied on. It revises policy condition 1(III) of Chapter 87 of ITC (HS) 2022, Schedule I, with immediate effect, under sections 3 and 5 of the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992 and paragraphs 1.02 and 2.01 of the Foreign Trade Policy, 2023. The existing column is the pre-1 January 1950 actual-user rule. The revised column, as the OCR could be read despite broken line wraps, says:

- Cars classified as vintage motor vehicles under the Explanation to sub-rule (1) of rule 81A of Chapter IIIA of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, as amended, are free for import by actual users.
- Policy conditions 1(I) and 1(II) of Chapter 87 do not apply to those vehicles.
- Cars that will ply on public roads remain subject to the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 and the rules under it.
- Those imported cars must comply with Chapter IIIA as amended from time to time.

The notification's effect line says the vintage classification was revised to align it with the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, 1989, as amended. No DGFT amendment after 7 February 2025 was checked through 22 October 2026. "Free for import" in this condition is a licensing classification for an actual user. It is not a customs-duty exemption and not a registration.

**What "vintage" means in the 2021 rules.** G.S.R. 492(E), quoted from the state-hosted gazette copy above, inserts Chapter IIIA. The Explanation to rule 81A(1) in the English text says a vintage motor vehicle, for L1 and L2 two-wheelers and M1 four-wheelers, is more than fifty years old from the date of its first registration after first sale, including a vehicle imported into India, kept in its original form, and not given a substantial overhaul, including any modification of the chassis, body shell, or engine. An application needs insurance, the rule 81 fee, a bill of entry for an import, and the old registration certificate if the vehicle was already registered in India. The English extract retrieved stops during rule 81B, which assigns a "VA" registration mark for a fresh registration. The Hindi text in the same file continues through validity, sale, and a use restriction. That use restriction was not recovered as clean English from the gazette, so its exact wording is unknown here. A PIB note of 16 July 2021, [PRID 1736506](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1736506&lang=2&reg=48), summarises the same amendment: vehicles more than fifty years old, in original form, without a substantial overhaul, with a VA series for fresh registration.

A listing that merely looks old does not meet this explanation. Age is counted from first registration after first sale. Original form and the absence of a substantial overhaul are conditions. Actual-user status is a DGFT condition. Chapter IIIA compliance is a further condition if the car will be used on a public road. The estimate therefore does not flip a foreign listing to "vintage importable" from age alone.

**Ordinary new and used imports.** [Notification No. 4 (RE-2001)/1997-2002, 31 March 2001](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/not04_1.pdf), read from DGFT's own site, is the historical insertion of used-vehicle and new-vehicle licensing notes: a used vehicle not older than three years, right-hand steering and controls, a kilometre speedometer, keep-left headlamp photometry, pre-shipment and arrival testing, a named port, and a five-year roadworthiness declaration; a new vehicle with right-hand drive, type-approval under CMVR rule 126, and named ports, with carve-outs for certification, defence, and manufacturer research. Notification No. 58/2024-25 still refers to policy conditions 1(I) and 1(II) as conditions that the vintage class escapes. Their exact words in the ITC (HS) 2022 schedule were not re-read on 22 October 2026. The 2001 notification is not, by itself, the 2026 eligibility rule. A foreign auction car stays in **unknown / requires review**. Transfer-of-residence baggage, diplomatic import, defence, racing, and test imports were not read and stay in that same status.

**Customs and auction costs.** No effective basic customs duty, agriculture-infrastructure cess, or social-welfare surcharge for heading 8703 was verified. The Union Budget 2026 customs rate-change annexure on [indiabudget.gov.in](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/cen/dojstru1.pdf) does not mention heading 8703 in the text extracted on 22 October 2026. Silence in a list of changes is not a rate. A third-party tariff mirror was not used. Duty, IGST, freight, buyer's premium, auction fees, port charges, broker charges, testing, and inland transport are entered amounts. There is no default premium percentage and no silent exchange rate. The scenario total is the arithmetic sum of the lines the user filled. It is not an assessment and not a statement that the vehicle can be registered.

## Finance and affordability

The Reserve Bank of India's circular [RBI/2024-25/18, 15 April 2024](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12663) (Key Facts Statement for Loans and Advances) was read for definitions only. It applies to retail and MSME term loans of banks and NBFCs. It defines the annual percentage rate as the annual cost of credit to the borrower, including the interest rate and all other charges associated with the credit facility. It defines an equated periodic instalment as a fixed repayment of principal and interest that completely amortises the loan, and it says a monthly instalment of that kind is an EMI. The circular requires a KFS and an APR computation sheet. Annex B of the circular, which illustrates the APR arithmetic, was not read. The circular text that was read sets no car-loan interest rate and no loan-to-value cap. No SBI or other lender rate was taken.

The estimate uses the nominal annual rate the user types. Let P be the amount financed, n the number of monthly instalments, i that nominal annual rate, and r = i/12. Interest each month is r times the opening balance, and the balance falls until it is repaid. The fixed instalment that does this is:

- if r = 0, EMI = P / n
- otherwise EMI = P · r · (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1)

The zero-rate case is a separate branch because the other expression divides by r. P is the quoted price line the user chooses to finance, plus any fee the user marks as financed, minus the user's down payment, and not below zero. Fees the user leaves unfinanced stay cash lines beside the EMI. An empty rate or an empty tenure means no EMI is shown. There is no default rate.

The nominal rate is not labeled as the APR. A zero nominal rate with an upfront fee can still be a positive APR under the RBI definition. The first release does not compute that APR. The user may type an APR taken from a lender's KFS and have it shown as an entered figure.

Affordability compares the EMI with a monthly budget the user types. If the budget is empty, the EMI stands alone and no salary test is applied. The user's down payment is the cash they plan to provide. An optional maximum financed share, also typed by the user, can flag the scenario when the financed amount exceeds that share. That flag is the user's own ceiling. It is not a lender's loan-to-value rule and not an eligibility decision.

## Rule pack for a later state calculator

A later calculator is one versioned pack per state, not a hard-coded table in the estimator. The pack has an instrument citation, a source URL, an effective date, and a `verified_on` date. `verified_on` means the operative text was checked on that date, including a search for later amendments. The pack quotes the tax base in the instrument's words, so a base that already includes GST is not grossed up again. It includes every rate that applies to the classes in scope: fuel, electric, age of an already registered vehicle, import, caps, and any cess levied on the tax. It lists the classes it does not cover.

Until `verified_on` is filled, that state's tax is unknown and the nationwide quote-assisted total is what the product shows. The 2017 Maharashtra slabs, the unread Delhi attachment, the Karnataka bill, and the 2021 vintage fee insertion are inputs to that future check. They are not a pack.

## First-release output

A domestic result has the quoted price, its basis label, each entered add-on, and a total only when every included line is either the quote or an amount the user entered. Missing registration or insurance is an open line, not zero.

A foreign result has the source price and currency, status **unknown / requires review**, and no rupee total. The hypothetical sheet lists only filled lines, names the user-supplied exchange rate and its date when a conversion is shown, and captions the sum as a scenario. The caption states that the sum is not a customs assessment and does not establish that the vehicle can be registered.

An affordability result has the nominal rate, tenure, down payment, fees, EMI, and, when the user typed one, the monthly budget and the gap between budget and EMI. It does not say the user qualifies for a loan.
